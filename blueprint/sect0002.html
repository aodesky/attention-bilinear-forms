Let \(S\) be symmetric, let \(\lambda _\pm \) be its sorted positive eigenvalues and absolute values of negative eigenvalues, padded with zeros to equal length, and let \(\mathcal P=1-\lVert \lambda _+-\lambda _-\rVert /\lVert S\rVert \). If \(\lambda _+\ne 0\) and \(\rho =\lVert \lambda _-\rVert /\lVert \lambda _+\rVert \), then

\[ \mathcal P=1-\sqrt{1-\frac{2\langle \lambda _+,\lambda _-\rangle }{\lVert S\rVert ^2}} \le 1-\frac{|1-\rho |}{\sqrt{1+\rho ^2}}, \]

with equality if and only if \(\lambda _-=\rho \lambda _+\).

Proof ▼

Since \(\lVert S\rVert ^2=\lVert \lambda _+\rVert ^2+\lVert \lambda _-\rVert ^2\), expanding \(\lVert \lambda _+-\lambda _-\rVert ^2\) gives the equality. The triangle inequality gives \(\lVert \lambda _+-\lambda _-\rVert \ge |1-\rho |\lVert \lambda _+\rVert \), with equality if and only if one list is a nonnegative multiple of the other; divide by \(\lVert S\rVert =\sqrt{1+\rho ^2}\lVert \lambda _+\rVert \).

If \(L\in M_N(\mathbb {R})\) has rank at most \(n\), the symmetric part of \(L\) has at most \(n\) positive and at most \(n\) negative eigenvalues, counted with multiplicity.

Proof ▼

The quadratic form of \(S\) vanishes on \(\ker L\), of dimension at least \(N-n\). The span of the eigenvectors with positive eigenvalues meets \(\ker L\) only in \(0\), so its dimension \(n_+\) is at most \(n\). For \(n_-\), apply the same argument to \(-L\).

Let \(Q,K\colon C\to H\) with \(\dim H=n\) and \(\dim C=N\). If the stacked map \(J=(Q,K)\colon C\to H\oplus H\) is surjective, the symmetric form \(S(x,y)=\tfrac 12(\langle Kx,Qy\rangle +\langle Qx,Ky\rangle )\) has signature \((n_+,n_-,n_0)=(n,n,N-2n)\). In Lean the signature is read off the Gram matrix of \(S\) in an arbitrary basis of \(C\).

Proof ▼

The Gram matrix of \(2S\) is the symmetric part of \(2K^TQ\), which has rank at most \(n\), so Lemma 3 gives \(n_\pm \le n\). The radical of \(S\) is \(\ker J\): if \(S(x,\cdot )=0\) then \(B(Jx,\cdot )\) vanishes on \(J(C)=H\oplus H\) for the nondegenerate form \(B((a,b),(a',b'))=\langle a,b'\rangle +\langle b,a'\rangle \). By surjectivity \(\dim \ker J=N-2n\), so \(S\) has rank \(2n=n_++n_-\), which forces \(n_+=n_-=n\).
