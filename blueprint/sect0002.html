Let \(S\) be symmetric, let \(\lambda _\pm \) be its sorted positive eigenvalues and absolute values of negative eigenvalues, padded with zeros to equal length, and let \(\mathcal P=1-\lVert \lambda _+-\lambda _-\rVert /\lVert S\rVert \). If \(\lambda _+\ne 0\) and \(\rho =\lVert \lambda _-\rVert /\lVert \lambda _+\rVert \), then

\[ \mathcal P=1-\sqrt{1-\frac{2\langle \lambda _+,\lambda _-\rangle }{\lVert S\rVert ^2}} \le 1-\frac{|1-\rho |}{\sqrt{1+\rho ^2}}, \]

with equality if and only if \(\lambda _-=\rho \lambda _+\).

Proof ▼

The equality comes from expanding a square, and the inequality is the reverse triangle inequality. The entries of \(\lambda _+\) and \(\lambda _-\) are the absolute values of the eigenvalues of \(S\), padded with zeros, so \(\lVert S\rVert ^2=\lVert \lambda _+\rVert ^2+\lVert \lambda _-\rVert ^2\). Expanding gives \(\lVert \lambda _+-\lambda _-\rVert ^2=\lVert S\rVert ^2-2\langle \lambda _+,\lambda _-\rangle \), which is the equality. By the reverse triangle inequality, \(\lVert \lambda _+-\lambda _-\rVert \ge \bigl|\lVert \lambda _+\rVert -\lVert \lambda _-\rVert \bigr|=|1-\rho |\lVert \lambda _+\rVert \), with equality exactly when one list is a nonnegative multiple of the other, that is, when \(\lambda _-=\rho \lambda _+\). Dividing by \(\lVert S\rVert =\sqrt{1+\rho ^2}\, \lVert \lambda _+\rVert \) gives the inequality.

If \(L\in M_N(\mathbb {R})\) has rank at most \(n\), the symmetric part of \(L\) has at most \(n\) positive and at most \(n\) negative eigenvalues, counted with multiplicity.

Proof ▼

The quadratic form of \(S\) vanishes on \(K=\ker L\), a subspace of dimension at least \(N-n\), so \(K\) meets the span of the eigenvectors with positive eigenvalues only in \(0\). For \(x\in K\), \(2x^TSx=x^TLx+x^TL^Tx=0\). Let \(V_+\) be the span of the eigenvectors of \(S\) with positive eigenvalues, of dimension \(n_+\). A nonzero \(x\in V_+\cap K\) would have both \(x^TSx{\gt}0\) and \(x^TSx=0\), so \(V_+\cap K=0\). Hence \(n_++(N-n)\le N\), that is, \(n_+\le n\). The same argument with the eigenvectors for negative eigenvalues gives \(n_-\le n\).

Let \(H\) be a real inner product space of dimension \(n\), let \(\dim C=N\), and let \(Q,K\colon C\to H\) be linear. If the stacked map \(J=(Q,K)\colon C\to H\oplus H\) is surjective, the symmetric form \(S(x,y)=\tfrac 12(\langle Kx,Qy\rangle +\langle Qx,Ky\rangle )\) has signature \((n_+,n_-,n_0)=(n,n,N-2n)\). In Lean the signature is read off the Gram matrix of \(S\) in an arbitrary basis of \(C\).

Proof ▼

Lemma 3 gives \(n_+\le n\) and \(n_-\le n\), and surjectivity of \(J\) makes the rank of \(S\) equal to \(2n\), so both bounds are equalities. Choose a basis of \(C\) and an orthonormal basis of \(H\). The Gram matrix of \(2S\) is \(K^TQ+Q^TK\), the symmetric part of \(2K^TQ\), and \(K^TQ\) has rank at most \(n\) because it factors through \(H\); so Lemma 3 applies. For the rank, on \(E=H\oplus H\) the symmetric form \(B((a,b),(a',b'))=\langle a,b'\rangle +\langle b,a'\rangle \) is nondegenerate and satisfies \(B(Jx,Jy)=2S(x,y)\). So if \(S(x,\cdot )=0\), then \(B(Jx,\cdot )\) vanishes on \(J(C)=E\) and \(Jx=0\); conversely \(Jx=0\) gives \(S(x,\cdot )=0\). The radical of \(S\) is therefore \(\ker J\), which has dimension \(N-2n\) because \(J\) is surjective. Thus \(n_++n_-=2n\), and with \(n_\pm \le n\) this forces \(n_+=n_-=n\) and \(n_0=N-2n\).
