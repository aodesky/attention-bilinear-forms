For \(m\ge 1\), let \(\Lambda _m\) consist of the unit vectors \(\lambda _1\le \cdots \le \lambda _{2m}\) with \(\lambda _m{\lt}0{\lt}\lambda _{m+1}\). Set

\[ (\lambda _+)_j=\lambda _{2m+1-j},\qquad (\lambda _-)_j=-\lambda _j\quad (1\le j\le m). \]

Define

\[ \mathcal I=\lVert \lambda _+\rVert ^2-\lVert \lambda _-\rVert ^2,\quad C=\langle \lambda _+,\lambda _-\rangle ,\quad \mathcal P=1-\lVert \lambda _+-\lambda _-\rVert ,\quad (\iota \lambda )_i=-\lambda _{2m+1-i}. \]

For \(\lambda \in \Lambda _m\),

\[ \lVert \lambda _+\rVert ^2=\tfrac 12(1+\mathcal I),\qquad \lVert \lambda _-\rVert ^2=\tfrac 12(1-\mathcal I),\qquad 2\lVert \lambda _+\rVert \lVert \lambda _-\rVert =\sqrt{1-\mathcal I^2}, \]
\[ \lVert \lambda _+-\lambda _-\rVert ^2=1-2C,\qquad \mathcal P=1-\sqrt{1-2C},\qquad \sum _j((\lambda _+)_j+i(\lambda _-)_j)^2=\mathcal I+2iC. \]

Moreover, \(0\le 2C\le 1\) and \(0\le \mathcal P\le 1\).

Proof ▼

The entries of \(\lambda _+\) and \(\lambda _-\) are the absolute values of the entries of \(\lambda \), so \(\lVert \lambda _+\rVert ^2+\lVert \lambda _-\rVert ^2=\lVert \lambda \rVert ^2=1\). Together with the definition of \(\mathcal I\) this gives the first two identities, and multiplying them gives the third. Expanding \(\lVert \lambda _+-\lambda _-\rVert ^2=\lVert \lambda _+\rVert ^2+\lVert \lambda _-\rVert ^2-2\langle \lambda _+,\lambda _-\rangle \) gives \(1-2C\) and hence the formula for \(\mathcal P\). In the complex sum, the real part is \(\lVert \lambda _+\rVert ^2-\lVert \lambda _-\rVert ^2=\mathcal I\) and the imaginary part is \(2\langle \lambda _+,\lambda _-\rangle =2C\). Finally \(C\ge 0\) because the entries of \(\lambda _\pm \) are nonnegative, and \(1-2C=\lVert \lambda _+-\lambda _-\rVert ^2\ge 0\); these give the bounds on \(C\) and \(\mathcal P\).

The map \(\iota \) is an orthogonal, self-adjoint linear involution, preserves \(\Lambda _m\), and exchanges \(\lambda _+\) and \(\lambda _-\). Consequently \(\mathcal I\circ \iota =-\mathcal I\), \(C\circ \iota =C\) and \(\mathcal P\circ \iota =\mathcal P\). For \(\lambda \in \Lambda _m\),

\[ \lVert \lambda -\iota \lambda \rVert ^2=2\lVert \lambda _+-\lambda _-\rVert ^2,\qquad \mathcal P(\lambda )=1-\frac1{\sqrt2}\lVert \lambda -\iota \lambda \rVert . \]

In particular, \(\mathcal P(\lambda )=1\) if and only if \(\iota \lambda =\lambda \).

Proof ▼

The matrix of \(\iota \) has entries \(-\delta _{j,2m+1-i}\); it is symmetric and orthogonal, and \(\iota ^2\) is the identity. If \(\lambda \) is nondecreasing then so is \(\iota \lambda \), and the entries of \(\iota \lambda \) are those of \(-\lambda \), so \(\iota \) preserves \(\Lambda _m\). The positive entries of \(\iota \lambda \) are \(-\lambda _1\ge \cdots \ge -\lambda _m\), so \(\lambda _+(\iota \lambda )=\lambda _-(\lambda )\), and symmetrically \(\lambda _-(\iota \lambda )=\lambda _+(\lambda )\); the rules for \(\mathcal I\), \(C\) and \(\mathcal P\) follow. For the displacement, the \(j\)-th and \((2m+1-j)\)-th coordinates of \(\lambda -\iota \lambda \) both equal \(\lambda _j+\lambda _{2m+1-j}=(\lambda _+)_j-(\lambda _-)_j\) up to sign, so \(\lVert \lambda -\iota \lambda \rVert ^2=2\lVert \lambda _+-\lambda _-\rVert ^2\). The last claim follows from Lemma 7, since \(\mathcal P(\lambda )=1\) exactly when \(\lambda _+=\lambda _-\).

Let \(L\ne 0\) and \(\pi (L)=(a,b,c,d)\). The coordinates are nonnegative and sum to one. Their faces and vertices have the following fibers.

  1. \(a=0\) exactly when \(L\) is symmetric, and \(a=1\) exactly when \(L\) is antisymmetric.

  2. \(b=0\) exactly when \(S\) is semidefinite, and \(b=1\) exactly when \(L\) is symmetric and \(\alpha =\beta \).

  3. \(d=0\) exactly when \(\alpha _j\ge \beta _j\) for every \(j\), equivalently \(S=H+P\) with \(H\) symmetric with spectrum symmetric about zero and \(P\) positive semidefinite. The matrices may be chosen to commute with \(S\) and with each other, with \(\lVert P\rVert ^2=c\lVert L\rVert ^2\). Also, \(d=1\) exactly when \(L\) is symmetric negative semidefinite.

  4. \(c=0\) exactly when \(\beta _j\ge \alpha _j\) for every \(j\), equivalently \(S=H-P\) with the same conditions on \(H,P\). They may be chosen to commute with \(S\) and with each other, with \(\lVert P\rVert ^2=d\lVert L\rVert ^2\). Also, \(c=1\) exactly when \(L\) is symmetric positive semidefinite.

Proof ▼

Rescale so that \(\lVert L\rVert =1\). Partition: \(\lVert \alpha \rVert ^2+\lVert \beta \rVert ^2=\lVert S\rVert ^2\), and expanding \(\lVert \alpha -\beta \rVert ^2=\lVert (\alpha -\beta )_+\rVert ^2+\lVert (\alpha -\beta )_-\rVert ^2\) gives \(b+c+d=\lVert S\rVert ^2\); Lemma 5 adds \(a=\lVert T\rVert ^2\) to make \(1\). Facets: \(a=0\) exactly when \(T=0\). If \(S\) is indefinite then \(\langle \alpha ,\beta \rangle \ge \alpha _1\beta _1{\gt}0\), so \(b=0\) exactly when \(S\) is semidefinite. \(d=0\) exactly when \(\alpha _j\ge \beta _j\) for all \(j\); then pair orthonormal eigenvectors \(v_j\) (eigenvalue \(\alpha _j\)) and \(w_j\) (eigenvalue \(-\beta _j\)), and put \(H=\sum _j\beta _j(v_jv_j^T-w_jw_j^T)\) and \(P=S-H=\sum _j(\alpha _j-\beta _j)v_jv_j^T\). Then \(H\) has spectrum symmetric about zero, \(P\) is positive semidefinite with \(\lVert P\rVert ^2=\lVert (\alpha -\beta )_+\rVert ^2=c\), and all three matrices are diagonal in one eigenbasis. Conversely, if \(S=H+P\), the Courant–Fischer min-max theorem gives \(\lambda _k(S)\ge \lambda _k(H)\) for the \(k\)-th largest eigenvalues. With the symmetry of the spectrum of \(H\), this shows that for every \(t{\gt}0\), \(S\) has at most as many eigenvalues below \(-t\) as above \(t\). If \(\alpha _j{\lt}t{\lt}\beta _j\) for some \(j\), at least \(j\) eigenvalues lie below \(-t\) and at most \(j-1\) lie above \(t\), a contradiction. The \(c\) face follows by applying the \(d\) face to \(-L\), which exchanges \(\alpha \) with \(\beta \) and \(c\) with \(d\). Vertices: each vertex is the set where the other three coordinates vanish, so it follows from the facets; for example, \(c=1\) forces \(a=b=d=0\), so \(L\) is symmetric, \(S\) is semidefinite, and \(S\) cannot be negative semidefinite since then \(d=1\).
