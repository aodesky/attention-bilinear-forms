1 Attention functions and transformers

Throughout, \(C\) is a finite-dimensional real vector space. Smooth means \(C^\infty \), and \(\Delta _{k-1}\subset \mathbb {R}^k\) is the standard simplex.

Definition 1 Attention function
✓

  • Appendices.MainText.IsAttentionFunction

A smooth map \(a\colon C^k\to C\) is an attention function if there are a smooth \(\omega \colon C^k\to \Delta _{k-1}\), a smooth convex \(\phi \colon \mathbb {R}^k\to \mathbb {R}\) and a polynomial map \(\sigma \colon C^k\to \mathbb {R}^k\) with

\[ \omega =(\nabla \phi )\circ \sigma ,\qquad a(x_1,\ldots ,x_k)=\sum _{j=1}^k\omega _j(x)\, x_j. \]

The data \(\omega ,\phi ,\sigma \) are carried along with \(a\). A polynomial map is one whose components are polynomials in the coordinates of \(x_1,\ldots ,x_k\).

Lemma 1 Biaffine decomposition
✓
#

  • Appendices.MainText.biaffine_components
  • Appendices.MainText.biaffine_decomposition

Every polynomial \(P\colon C\times C\to \mathbb {R}\) of bidegree at most \((1,1)\) has a unique decomposition \(P(u,v)=B(u,v)+L(u)+R(v)+D\) with \(B\) bilinear, \(L,R\) linear and \(D\in \mathbb {R}\), namely

\[ D=P(0,0),\quad L(u)=P(u,0)-P(0,0),\quad R(v)=P(0,v)-P(0,0), \]
\[ B(u,v)=P(u,v)-P(u,0)-P(0,v)+P(0,0). \]
Proof ▼

The paper calls this lemma elementary and gives no proof; this sketch follows the Lean proof. In coordinates, \(P\) is a sum of monomials \(f(u)g(v)\) in which each of \(f\) and \(g\) is either the constant \(1\) or a single coordinate function. Substituting this sum into the four formulas shows that \(B\) is bilinear, \(L\) and \(R\) are linear and \(D\) is constant, and that \(B+L+R+D=P\). For uniqueness, evaluate an arbitrary decomposition at \((0,0)\), \((u,0)\) and \((0,v)\): this recovers \(D\), then \(L\) and \(R\), and then \(B\).

Let \(H\) be a finite-dimensional real vector space (the head space), and let a bilinear attention function on \(H\) have forms \(B_{k-1},\ldots ,B_0\), let \(L_j\colon H\to H^\vee \), \(L_j(v)(u)=B_j(u,v)\), assume \(B_0\) nondegenerate, and set \(T_d=L_0^{-1}L_d\). The function is RoPE-like if \(T_{d+e}=T_dT_e\) whenever \(d,e\ge 0\) and \(d+e{\lt}k\).

Proposition 1 Classification of RoPE-like attention functions

With \(B_0\) nondegenerate, the function is RoPE-like if and only if there is \(S\in \operatorname {End}(H)\) with \(T_d=S^d\) for all \(0\le d{\lt}k\). When \(S\) exists it is \(T_1=L_0^{-1}L_1\).

Proof ▼

Suppose the function is RoPE-like and put \(S=T_1\). Since \(L_d=L_0T_d\), the relation \(T_{d+e}=T_dT_e\) reads \(L_{d+e}=L_dL_0^{-1}L_e\); with \(e=1\) it gives \(L_{d+1}=L_dS\). Induction on \(d\) gives \(L_d=L_0S^d\), that is, \(T_d=S^d\). Conversely, if \(T_d=S^d\) then \(T_{d+e}=S^{d+e}=S^dS^e=T_dT_e\). Taking \(d=1\) in \(T_d=S^d\) shows that \(S=T_1\), so \(S\) is unique.

Let \(A_1,\ldots ,A_h\) be attention heads and \(V_1,\ldots ,V_h\colon C\to C\) linear maps, the value maps. The attention block is \(A=\sum _{i=1}^h(V_i\otimes \mathrm{id})\circ A_i\), that is, \(A(x)_k=\sum _iV_i(A_i(x)_k)\). For a map \(M\colon C\to C\), the transformer associated with \(A\) and \(M\) is \(T=T_M\circ T_A\), where \(T_A(x)_k=x_k+A(x)_k\) and \(T_M(y)_k=y_k+M(y_k)\). A single-head transformer is one with \(h=1\).

Proposition 2 Transformers are near-identity

Fix a norm on \(C\) and put \(\lVert x\rVert _\infty =\max _k\lVert x_k\rVert \) for \(x\in C^\ell \). Let \(T\) be the transformer associated with an attention block \(A=\sum _i(V_i\otimes \mathrm{id})\circ A_i\) and a map \(M\) with \(\lVert M(y)\rVert \le L\lVert y\rVert \) for all \(y\in C\), and put \(V=\sum _i\lVert V_i\rVert \) (operator norms). Then \(\lVert T(x)-x\rVert _\infty \le (L+V+LV)\lVert x\rVert _\infty \) for all \(x\in C^\ell \).

Proof ▼

An attention function returns an average of its inputs, so the attention block has norm at most \(V\lVert x\rVert _\infty \), and the bound follows from the form of \(T\). Since the attention weights lie in the simplex, \(\lVert \sum _j\omega _jx_j\rVert \le \sum _j\omega _j\lVert x_j\rVert \le \max _j\lVert x_j\rVert \). So every component of a head \(A_i\) has norm at most \(\lVert x\rVert _\infty \) (the first component is \(x_1\) itself), and \(\lVert A(x)_k\rVert \le \sum _i\lVert V_i\rVert \lVert x\rVert _\infty =V\lVert x\rVert _\infty \). The \(k\)-th component of \(T(x)-x\) is \(A(x)_k+M(x_k+A(x)_k)\), whose norm is at most \(V\lVert x\rVert _\infty +L(1+V)\lVert x\rVert _\infty =(L+V+LV)\lVert x\rVert _\infty \).

Let \(\widehat C=C\oplus \mathbb {R}e\) and \(\iota (x)=x+e\). For every single-head QKV transformer \(T\colon C^\ell \to C^\ell \), with scores \(\langle Q(x_k+p_k),K(x_j+p_j)\rangle /\sqrt n\), there is a single-head bilinear transformer \(\widehat T\) on \(\widehat C\) with the same potentials such that \(\widehat T(\iota x_1,\ldots ,\iota x_\ell )=\iota ^\ell (T(x_1,\ldots ,x_\ell ))\).

Proof ▼

The new coordinate \(e\) carries the position vectors: on the chart \(\iota (C)=C+e\) the QKV scores become bilinear forms, and everything else is extended by ignoring the new coordinate. For the score of position \(k\) on position \(j\le k\), define a bilinear form on \(\widehat C\) by

\[ \widehat B(u+\alpha e,v+\beta e)=\tfrac 1{\sqrt n}\bigl(\langle Qv,Ku\rangle +\alpha \langle Qv,Kp_j\rangle +\beta \langle Qp_k,Ku\rangle +\alpha \beta \langle Qp_k,Kp_j\rangle \bigr). \]

Expanding the QKV score shows that \(\widehat B(\iota x_j,\iota x_k)\) is the score of \(x_k\) on \(x_j\). So on the chart the two heads have the same scores, hence the same attention weights \(\omega _j\), and since the weights sum to one, \(\widehat a_k(\iota x_1,\ldots ,\iota x_k)=\sum _j\omega _j(x_j+e)=\iota (a_k(x_1,\ldots ,x_k))\). Put \(\widehat V(u+\alpha e)=V(u)\) and \(\widehat M(u+\alpha e)=M(u)\). Then \(\iota (x)+\widehat V(\iota z)=\iota (x+Vz)\) and \(\iota (x)+\widehat M(\iota x)=\iota (x+M(x))\), which give \(\widehat T\circ \iota ^\ell =\iota ^\ell \circ T\).

The paper leaves implicit that the new attention weights are smooth and lie in the simplex on all of \(\widehat C^k\), not only on the chart. The Lean proof checks this using \(\widehat\sigma (u_1+\alpha _1e,\ldots ,u_k+\alpha _ke)=\sigma (x)\) with \(x_j=u_j+(\alpha _j-1)p_j\).
